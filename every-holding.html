    Why it is a range

    The listed shares are not in doubt: the filings give the count and the market gives the price, so those lines are exact at each close. The doubt is in two kinds of holding. Some shares are his on paper but restricted or not yet bought, and the publishers disagree on whether to count them. And two private companies have no share price, only funding rounds and private trades, which put very different values on them.

    So the low end counts only what is plainly his today, with each private company at its lower published value. The high end adds the disputed shares and each private company at its higher value. The truth, by any honest method, sits somewhere between.

    What the two switches do

    Turn off the 2018 Tesla shares and the options, and the high end drops to $878.1B. That is close to what a publisher that leaves them out would show. Turn them on and you have what one that counts them in full would show. Why Forbes and Bloomberg disagree goes through each choice.

    How it stays up to date

    The listed lines move with every US close we record on the day by day page, and with Calculate now on the home page. The share counts change only when a new filing does, and the private valuations when a new round or trade is reported, each with its date and source.

    How much is Elon Musk’s Tesla stake worth?

    At the close on 15 October 2026, the 413,152,109 Tesla shares in his trust were worth $149.7 billion at $362.44 a share. The 286,428,773 restricted shares from 2018 add $103.8 billion if they are counted.

    How much is Elon Musk’s SpaceX stake worth?

    At the close on 15 October 2026, the 4,766,475,230 SpaceX shares held by his trusts were worth $708.4 billion at $148.62 a share. His 350,000,000 options add up to $52 billion more, before the cost of using them. xAI and X are inside SpaceX, so they are part of this figure.
